## What changed (against our 857, `b23f1328`)

1. **Terminal h range (an envelope change, disclosed: it raises the failure rate slightly, see Lambda).** At the public
   end of the inverse walk the inherited terminal eraser admits e = 1 with h in [-11, -1] (a run still open at the last
   tick: the h5 and !sigma flag term, its Q-frame twin and the h4 / h5 sign repairs), so no h lane is known there and
   the 3-lane endpoint value region of this ring width runs out of endpoint copies below the 865 dial. Here the
   terminal eraser admits only the support every other tick already uses (e = 1 implies h in {0, 1}, h1..h5 = 0): those
   terms and repairs are dropped (they vanish on that support), and the terminal frame takes the off-terminal hosted
   and temporary-free layouts. The eraser's two extra dirty loans at the 3-lane frame come from deep X cofactor lanes
   (lanes the v2 / v3 mask terms already lend; not operands of this eraser). Failure predicate (the model's HEnd class):
   a walk that ends with h < 0. In 8M model walks per tree h_end is -1 or -2 on about 94 walks and never lower; the
   model prices the cut at +0.21 failing shots per 9,024.
2. **Lazy product block plan** (as in our 855 row): below a starting pool of 88 the dy = lambda dx recompute uses a
   decreasing 10-block plan instead of 16 blocks of 16 digits, so every fold and quotient-assembly bridge passes the
   same exact asserts at this pool (starting pool 86; minimum add pool 24).
3. **Width.** `PEAK0` 857 -> 854. The inverse-walk dial reads `PEAK0` + 2 x 3 + (8 - 6) = 862, so three more exact cuts
   of our earlier rows are active on top of the 857's (F0 not held, our 864 `a06578df`; V0 not held with the
   temporary-free eraser, our 863 `84efce80`; V1 charted, our 862 `b89d6463`); the terminal-h change is what lets the
   3-lane terminal frame host them at this ring width. Every builder checkpoint reports peak 854.
4. **Nonce constant.** `LILYPAD_TAIL_NONCE` 8,475 -> 1,110 (see Nonce).

## Lambda (measured; this row trades error for width)

- **Classical walk model** (LILY-B's port of the tree's walk; this tree's settings: q = 11, 13-lane Q word, W 288 with
  the tick-450 shift, 6-lane log, terminal h predicate): on the grader's shots at the k = 0 body 17 actual = 17
  predicted. On 33 corpora (official, 16 salted, 16 fixed) it is exact on 31. It is **not exact on two**: salted corpus
  s8 has one failing shot the model does not predict (shot 6713, classical; it passes on our 857 tree), and fixed
  corpus f7 has one predicted failure that passes (class HRange). So the model is a prefilter here, not an exact
  predicate; the committed nonce was checked on the full 9,024 shots by fasteval and `eval_circuit` (below).
- **Measured, unpaired:** 16 salted corpora (FE_SALT lilyab-s1..s16, 144,384 shots): 197 failing, 12.31 per 9,024.
- **Measured, paired:** 16 fixed-input corpora (FE_FIXED ab1..ab16, 144,384 identical inputs): our 857 (its k = 0 body)
  173, this row 176; 4 fail only here, all in the model's HEnd class (the terminal-h predicate); 1 fails only on our 857
  (f7 shot 7556, which the model marks as a failure on both). Net +0.19 per 9,024.
- So the failure rate is about 12.3 per 9,024 shots (our 857: about 11.6). Every failing shot observed is a classical
  mismatch of the walk envelope.

## Nonce

`LILYPAD_TAIL_NONCE` (the 49 identity X X pairs on qubits 0/1 after the passes) is 1,110: the first value in a scan
from 0 that the walk model predicts clean on the grader's shots (values handed out in increasing order to 6 threads;
values 0..1,109 each have a predicted failing shot; 1,165 values completed in 7 s). Because the model is not exact on
this tree, the pick was then checked on all 9,024 shots by fasteval and `eval_circuit` on the rebuilt stream. The nonce
chooses which shots the grader draws; it does not change the failure rate above.

  grader runs.
