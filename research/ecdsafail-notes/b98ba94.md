# 718 qubits, 4.3% fewer Toffoli: the 718 frogdrop payload walk with the always-zero quotient overflow subset removed

**Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), run in Claude Code (main session plus build and design agents).
**Result (exact local replica of `eval_circuit`, all 9024 shots OK, 0 classical, 0 phase, 0 ancilla failures):**
718 qubits × 284,765,142 average executed Toffoli = **204,461,371,956**.
This is a low-qubit Pareto point, not a frontier score. It has fewer qubits than any submission on the board when it was
submitted. At the same qubit count it improves our graded 718 (b4031322) by 4.3% Toffoli; it improves our 725
(1af89a00) by 7 qubits and 11.3% Toffoli.
**Base:** nasqret's Q730 submission bef99551 (source commit `2b258d44`). That source is our Q732 payload walk (51cf3ca4)
plus nasqret's zero parking and a first K cut. Harness unchanged.
**Change against `2b258d44`:**
- machine constants: `N` 286 → 281, `K` 28 → 23, `KP` 30 → 24;
- the computed quotient: 26 → 24 bits, removing the two overflow lanes (`QB` stays 24; §2 item 12);
- the Fiat–Shamir nonce: 6 → 50;
- a re-carved column pool, a 24-lane payload digit, per-column counter parking, an 8-lane size register, a mod-256
  estimate start and a payload decoder change, in `frogdrop.rs`, `frogdrop_col.rs`, `frogdrop_sched.rs`, `mask.rs`,
  `payload.rs` and `pointadd_frogdrop.rs` (§2);
- test-only edits in `tests_frogdrop.rs`, `tests_q730_ring286_k28.rs` and `tests_q731_zero_parking.rs`.

λ ≈ 5 is traded for qubits and stated in §4.

## ELI5

The circuit divides by dx with a Euclid walk. At each step it guesses the quotient from the leading bits of two numbers,
then corrects the guess. The guess reads K bits of one number from the walk's main register (the ring) and KP bits of
the other through a scratch pool shared by every column. Each bit of K is a ring lane and each bit of KP is a pool lane.

Our 725 cut K to 23, its floor: below that the guess fails almost every time. Here we measured KP and found it has
slack. Every KP from 30 down to 25 gives the same failure rate. The pool, though, was laid out for 40 lanes. One step
held an 8-lane copy of a size value at the moment every other lane was busy, so it could not shrink. We removed that copy
and re-packed the pool's other roles. Two smaller cuts take it one lane further: the payload digit register is 24 lanes
instead of 26, and the idle counter parks only the bits a column can need. Then the divisor's size register shrinks from
9 to 8 bits, because the divisor's bit-length never exceeds 255 after the first column. The pool now fits in 33 lanes:
7 qubits fewer than the 725 at about the same error. Finally, the quotient estimate stops computing two quotient bits
that are always zero on the shots this circuit can pass, which saves 4.3% Toffoli. The rework also lets a payload decoder use more clean lanes, which saves Toffoli.

## 1. What KP is and where the 5 qubits come from

Each column estimates the Euclid quotient with a reciprocal pipeline P from two heads:
- the top K bits of the ring value Z (the numerator head, at the ring top);
- the top KP bits of the Q value (the divisor head `Yh`), whose remainder ρ < (Yh | 1) uses KP + 1 lanes: the 8-lane
  spare size register plus KP − 7 pool lanes.

The pool is `pool_size = 17 + (KP + 1 − 8)` lanes, so one lane per bit of KP.

The source's classical walk predictor (`payload_predict_nonce`, which evaluates the estimate with the build's K, KP, QB
and TOT) gives about the same failure rate from KP 30 down to 24 at K = 23, then roughly doubles per bit:

| KP | 30 | 29 | 28 | 27 | 26 | 25 | 24 | 23 | 22 | 21 | 20 |

| model λ (failing shots per 9,024) | ≈ 5.0 | 5.03 | 4.97 | 5.02 | 4.97 | 5.07 | 5.53 | 7.08 | 12.1 | 21.5 | 40.0 |

(KP 30–26 on one set of 60 nonce shot sets; KP 25–20 on another set of 60, paired across those KP.) KP = 24 is the
practical floor.

## 2. The pool re-carve

Before this change the builder asserted "pool too small for the first-HT temps" at KP = 29. A replay of the 730 circuit
that recorded, op by op, which qubits are nonzero on any shot showed why: in short bursts every one of the 730 lanes is
busy at once, so the pool cannot lose a lane without a step needing one lane less. The changes:

1. **Stage 2 (first-HT shots arriving in the switched layout).** The old path probed bl(Q) into an 8-lane temp U,
   swapped it with sy, and later cleared U against T = bl(ring top). Now:
   - set the gate js;
   - run the gated swap;
   - compute T = bl(top) into sx;
   - clear sy with sy ^= js·T (on those shots sy = bl(X) = T, the same identity the old U ^= T used);
   - probe bl(Q) straight into the cleared sy (`probe_gated` needs a zero target only on gated shots);
   - clear js.
   
   U is gone, and so are its 8 controlled swaps.
2. **Stages 14 and 15.** The stage-14 size temp and the stage-15 counter carries use the sz lanes, which are clean after
   stage 9.
3. **`carve`.** tmp, tmp2, the probe prefixes and js come from the ρ region of the pool (js never lands on the
   middle-zero ρ lane for KP 25–30). The compare helpers take the remaining non-probe lanes there. A new assert checks
   that the stage-2 compare chain stays clear of sx and sy.
4. **Payload HT narrowing.** It needed 30 lanes. It now runs before the switch flag is computed, which is safe because it
   never reads that flag, and it borrows the switch-flag lane. Its counter parking runs only on columns that can hold
   finished shots; elsewhere it was the identity.
5. **Payload digit.** Its stepping flag rides on the switch-flag lane: HR flips it in place, and HT uncomputes the switch
   flag from (ph, sz), holds its own flag there, and recomputes the switch flag afterwards. Its scratch now needs 31
   lanes (the 24-bit digit of item 7, its helpers and zeros) instead of 38.
6. **Payload decoder banks.** Below k = 8 the digit's decoder banks had only 2 lanes. They now also take lanes that are
   clean at that step: the digit's own unwritten bits, its helpers and zero pads. The size-gate chain takes those lanes
   too, and falls back to a compact compare on dirty ring-top lanes when still short (new helper `mask::ge_const_need`).
   Turning this off alone costs 5.57M of the 5.59M saved per payload traversal, about 11M over the circuit. It works at
   any KP, including 725.

7. **Payload digit register, 26 → 24 lanes.** Each payload digit e_j = ⌊d / r_j⌋ is at most the level's quotient q_j,
   since d < r_{j−1}. The model already counts any shot with a quotient of 2^24 or more as failing (its "q0" class, at
   every level including the last), so on every model-clean shot e_j < 2^24 and 24 lanes suffice. A shot with such a
   quotient fails here even where the parent's overflow lanes might have carried it; those shots are inside the stated
   λ (the q0 class is about 0.15 of it). This also saves about 3.5M emitted Toffoli per payload traversal.
8. **Counter parking, trimmed per column.** A finished shot's idle counter can only have grown since the first column
   where shots can finish (a shot finishing elsewhere is a model "envelope" failure). On the columns that both may switch
   and hold finished shots, the counter fits in 5 bits, so the map and the HT narrowing park 5 counter lanes there
   instead of 7. That frees the 2 map lanes those columns were short of at KP = 24.

9. **Size register sz, 9 → 8 lanes.** sz = bl(Z) is at most 255 in every column but the first, since r_2 < p/2 and
   |t_j| ≤ p/2. `col_pars` caps the envelope at 255 from column 1 on, and the predictor applies the same cap. Column 0
   never switches, so it borrows the switch-flag lane as sz's ninth bit. The masked swaps, sign reads, H51 certificate,
   rotations, probes and narrows were already width-generic. The swap decoder keeps 4 prefixes, so with an 8-bit sz it
   needs only 4-control dirty toggles.
10. **Estimate start held mod 256.** On stepping shots sz + sy is 228–257, so with the start offset at −220 every start
    index is non-negative. The four warm-up compares are then constant and skipped, and the start decoder is exact
    mod 256 over the 83 iterations (asserted). This saves about 2.4M Toffoli per payload traversal.
11. **Payload start s = sz + sy (9 bits).** The digit stage has exactly one lane beyond its 31-lane scratch. It holds
    the sum's ninth bit, or the stepping flag in column 0.

12. **Quotient computed in 24 bits, not 26 (the change since b4031322).** P used to compute q0 with 26 bits, the
    base QB = 24 plus "two paid overflow lanes". The model already counts any shot with q0 ≥ 2^24 as failing (its q0
    class, at every level), and the 24-lane payload digit of item 7 already breaks such shots. So on every shot this
    circuit can pass, the top two quotient bits are zero. P now computes 24 bits:
    - the top stored subset {24, 25} is gone, so each column has 12 quotient subsets instead of 13;
    - its two former tail iterations become plain head iterations, identical for q0 < 2^24.
    
    That removes one subset's switch replays, adds and payload transitions from every column, with no extra lanes and
    no new failures. One payload traversal drops from 106.32M to 101.73M emitted Toffoli; the whole circuit drops by
    12.9M (−4.29%).

With these the map already fits the 33-lane pool: fixed 22 + extension 2 + parked counter 5 + one more lane = 30.

On the 719 layout, KP = 23 also gave 718 qubits and passed the same hunts, but it netted only −0.03% and raised λ to
about 7.1. This build takes the lane from the size register instead, at λ ≈ 5.6.

**Equivalence first.** The re-carve was built and checked at KP = 30 before any cut. On the same 16,384 random divisions
and 4,096 random point additions as the unchanged 725 source, it failed exactly the same inputs. In the exact replica at
the 725's nonce it failed 4 shots, the number the model predicts for that op stream, and the first failing shot is the
first one the model lists.

## 4. Failure budget

This build keeps the 725's trade of error for qubits, for the low-qubit frontier. The quotient estimate's model failure
rate is λ ≈ 5.5 per 9,024 shots over the first 238 nonce shot sets of this op stream (5.6 over all 718 positions for
b4031322), against 0.20 for nasqret's K = 28. Dropping the overflow subset does not change it: the model already counts
those shots as failures. λ stays far below the PR 419 contract's 18 (§7).

| check | inputs | result |
|---|---|---|
| traversal hunt, random divisors (same inputs as the 725) | 16,384 | 5 bad, the same 5 as the 720, 719 and 718 and a subset of the 725's 7, all model-predicted estimate failures |
| targeted hunt (inputs that finish while a probe reaches the counter lanes) | 512 | 0 bad |
| end-to-end point additions (same inputs as the 725) | 4,096 | 1 bad, the same input that fails on the 725 |
| exact replica of `eval_circuit` at nonce 50 | 9,024 | 0 classical, 0 phase, 0 ancilla |

No new failure kinds appeared: phase or ancilla garbage shows only on shots that are already classically wrong.

**Nonce.** The grader rejects a circuit if any of its 9,024 shots fails, and at λ ≈ 5 only about 1% of shot sets
have no failure. The nonce (the qubit of the final identity X·X pair, which changes only the op stream's Fiat–Shamir hash)
was therefore searched with the source's model, in order from position 0. Positions 0–49 are not clean; 50 is, and the
replica confirms it (the scan was stopped at 238 positions, where 397 is the only other clean one). The circuit is the same for every nonce, so the choice does not change the true
failure rate stated above.

## 5. Reproduce

`cargo run --release --bin build_circuit` (or `./benchmark.sh`). The build holds every op in memory, about 77 GB, as
does `eval_circuit`. `LOWQ_EXACT_STREAM_PATH=<file>` streams the ops to disk instead (byte-identical to `write_ops`).

The 9,024-shot result comes from fasteval, a parallel exact replica of `eval_circuit`
(https://github.com/BitWonka/ecdsafail-tools, `fasteval/`). It matched the grader to the last digit on our 728 and 725
entries and every earlier one we compared. The checks are the `#[ignore]` tools in `tests_frogdrop.rs`:
- `frogdrop_traversal_hunt` with `FROGDROP_BATCHES=256`, and with `FROGDROP_HITS=1 FROGDROP_BATCHES=8`;
- `payload_e2e_hunt` with `FROGDROP_BATCHES=64`;
- `payload_predict_nonce` with `FROGDROP_OPSFILE=<ops.bin>`, `FROGDROP_SCAN=<end>` and, new here, `FROGDROP_SCAN0=<start>`
  to split a scan across processes. With no scan set it now prints the predicted failing shot indices.
