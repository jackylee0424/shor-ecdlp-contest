# frogstrip: a ±1-digit 2-adic walk with a strip barrel (948 qubits, 3.03M Toffoli)

**Model:** Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic), run in Claude Code: the main session plus Claude Opus 5.5 sub-agents for the
design study, the build and the lever round.
**Result (official `ecdsafail run`, all 9024 shots OK, 0 classical / 0 phase / 0 ancilla):** 948 qubits ×
3,034,861 average executed Toffoli = **2,877,048,228**.
**Base:** frogtail at 917 × 3,720,185 = 3,411,409,645 (submission 35f51358). frogstrip keeps its shell, its
carry-pool arithmetic, sign compression, finished-shot absorption and the tape-free regenerating inverse, and
replaces the walk's tick structure. Not a frontier score: a low-qubit Pareto point.

## ELI5

frogtail computes 1/dx with a Stehlé–Zimmermann 2-adic Euclid walk that ticks once per halving: 556 ticks, every
tick paying for an add, a shelf swap and the counters, whether or not it does anything. Half of its ticks add
nothing: either the quotient digit is 0, or the tick is an idle halving that only strips a trailing zero.

frogstrip removes both kinds of empty tick.
- **No zero digits.** Every SZ quotient can be written with ±1 digits only, so every tick adds or subtracts.
- **Idle halvings move into a barrel.** After a step's last digit, the trailing zeros are stripped by a small
  4-way barrel shift of the active ring, up to 3 per tick, in the same tick.

A tick now does 1 to 4 halvings instead of exactly 1, and a walk takes 300 ticks instead of 556. Each tick costs a
little more, for the barrel and an erase that now runs every tick, but there are about half as many.

## Design comparison

| design (module in its code) | how the inversion stores its state | qubits | average Toffoli |
|---|---|---|---|
| **frogstrip** (`frogstrip.rs`) | SZ 2-adic walk with ±1 digits only; trailing zeros stripped by a segment rot4 barrel (1–4 halvings a tick, 300 ticks); value + cofactor packed on two 315-lane rings; no tape: digits measured forward, regenerated backward | **948** | **3.03M** |
| **frogtail** (`frogtail.rs`, submission 35f51358) | the same SZ walk with 0/1 digits, one halving a tick (556 ticks); two 308-lane rings; same tape-free inverse | 917 | 3.72M |
| **froghop-single** (`froghop_single.rs`) | full-quotient Euclid; remainder + cofactor packed on 2 × 314 lanes; one up-and-down hop per step to align the divisor | 940 | 8.74M |
| **Leapfrog** (`leapfrog.rs`) | 2-adic walk on two odd rails with a 5-bit letter per tick on a tape and the payload pair updated every tick | 1,243 | 0.80M |

- **Against frogtail.** The walks are 300 ticks instead of 556. The forward walk is 18% cheaper and the inverse 17%,
  even with the barrel on every tick. The price is qubits:
  - the rings grow from 308 to 315 lanes each, because shots now drift further apart in halvings at any tick;
  - an exponent counter holds the per-shot strip count.
- **Against froghop-single.** 8 more qubits at a third of the Toffoli.
- **Against Leapfrog.** 295 fewer qubits at 3.8× the Toffoli.

## 1. The walk

Two rings A (active) and B each hold a value and its cofactor, with A.v·A.c + B.v·B.c = p, as in frogtail. A's frame
offset moves one lane a tick (the classical halving); the strip barrel adds the data-dependent extra halvings.

**Tick t:**
1. **Step end:** e = [j ≤ −1] ∧ A.v odd. Then j ← −j, parity, a controlled swap of the shelves over the
   sign-compressed widths, and e is erased as the new A.c's low bit.
2. **Digit:** a = [j ≥ 1] ∧ B.v odd. The sign is (A.v[1] ⊕ B.v[1]) ⊕ [j = 1]: it keeps (A.v ± B.v)/2 odd while digits
   remain and makes it even on the step's last digit. A.v ±= B.v and B.c ∓= A.c, controlled by a.
3. **Halve:** relabel A, sign fixes at the value/cofactor boundary, j −= 1.
4. **Strip:** k = min(ν(A.v), 3) from A.v's low 3 bits, under G = [j ≤ 0]. A segment rot4 (three controlled
   reflections) shifts ring A by k over the live lanes, and the lanes that wrap take their new owner's sign (4 T).
   j and the exponent counter E move by k.

**Counters:**
- **The strip pattern is fixed.** It is min(z, 3 + 4i), so k = (−j) mod 4 on strip ticks: the barrel's controls are
  recomputed from j with CNOTs and erased by measurement.
- **j + t + S is always odd** (S = strips so far), so j's low bit follows from the tick parity and E's low bit, and j
  is stored as j >> 1.
- **The completion flag does double duty.** The digit's [j = 1] equals [j = 0] after the halve, so the same flag
  serves as the erase's control and as the strip's trigger.

**Absorption:** finished shots are absorbed from tick 256 (frogtail's mechanism): one last step end moves the
cofactor to ring B, and the shot freezes with j constant, no digit and no strip. After the walk, one barrel
rebuilds C' = C·2^sh, with sh from the exponent counter.

## 2. Tape-free inverse

The forward walk X-measures each digit. The inverse walk replays a regenerating tick backwards: at a step's
completion it rebuilds the step's digit word from the cofactors' low bits, and a phase fix cancels each digit's
measurement. The digit erase is frogtail's, adapted to ±1 digits:
- **The word:** the digit register holds A.v[1] ⊕ B.v[1] for each digit plus a marker 1 below the word (set by CNOT
  from the step-end flag). With that, (2D + 1)·b ≡ L (mod 2^(g+1)), with b = A.c >> s' odd and L = B.c's low bits. So
  the erase is a multiply by +b, a rotation by g − 1 and a masked XOR of L, with no correction step.
- **Every tick:** completion happens when t + S is odd, which differs per shot, so the erase runs on every tick.
- **Normalizer:** its swap chains move only the lanes b's bits 0..g need.

## 3. Shell and arithmetic

The point addition is frogtail's shell: two divisions, measurement-based erasure of dy and λ, and products modulo
p = 2^256 − c with c = 2^32 + 977, on a carry pool made of every free qubit below the peak. The arithmetic is new:
- **Folds by table.** Each fold adds a constant chosen by 2–4 control bits. Each addend bit is a Boolean function of
  those controls (algebraic normal form monomials by AND, the rest by CNOT), followed by one carry chain.
- **Four doublings per fold.** c − 1 = 16·(61 + 2^28), so 16·z mod p is a relabel of the top 4 bits plus one 64-lane
  table fold. This replaces the 871 single doublings and halvings in the shell.
- **One fold per Horner step.**
  - **Add then halve:** z ← (z ± y)/2 becomes (z~ + δ·c)/2 + h·2^255, with δ = ±k − h and h = z~₀ ⊕ k. That is one
    table fold, with h on the freed top lane, and the carry k is erased by the usual 32-lane compare on the halved
    value's lanes.
  - **Add then double:** the add's overflow fold merges into the next doubling's fold, and the top bit is
    relabelled into lane 0.
- **Streaming digits.** Division 1's tail product takes its digits from dy, and ndy's tail takes them from λ·2^308.
  Each digit is X-measured right after its last use, and its lane joins the carry pool.
- **λ-fix uncompute as a functional inverse.** Above step 48 the tail's inverse is built from forward chunked
  operations. A partial sum can hit 0 only via the unique ±1 representation of −1, with probability 2^−(k+2) at
  step k, which is at most 2^−49 a tail.
- **Inherited:** signed-digit Horner products, the chunked carry bank and the dy recompute's phase fix, all
  frogtail's.

## 4. Cost

Average executed Toffoli per phase (64-shot profile of the submitted circuit, total 3,034,611 against the
evaluator's 3,034,861):

| phase | frogtail (35f51358) | frogstrip |
|---|---|---|
| forward walk (each of 2) | 553.6k | 456.1k |
| inverse walk (each of 2) | 851.4k | 703.5k |
| λ = C·dy·2^−T (tail product) | 145.7k | 103.8k |
| dy recompute + phase fix + uncompute | 230.6k | 194.8k |
| x' = λ² − dx − 3ox | 118.2k | 93.7k |
| ndy = −λ·dx' | 133.4k | 95.5k |
| λ recompute + phase fix + uncompute | 280.2k | 227.1k |
| **total** | **3,720,185** | **3,034,861** |

The walks are 76% of the Toffoli.

Per walk tick, roughly:
- **Forward:** adds ~720, strip barrel ~460, swap ~308, control ~35.
- **Inverse:** the same, plus the erase at ~920.

**Peak (948):** dy 256, rings 2 × 315, digit register 21, counters and flags, scratch, and the carry pool. The
inverse walk's cofactor add needs about 24 pool lanes to keep its chunk plan.

## 5. Failure budget

The walk's schedule is classical:
- ring width 315;
- 300 ticks;
- strip and add envelopes from the per-tick maxima of 1.8M simulated walks;
- a digit register of 21 lanes, which caps a step's gap at 20.

A shot fails when its walk leaves this envelope. On 4M walks of the integer model, that comes to about **2.5 failing
divisions per 9024-shot run**: about 2.0 from the gap cap and about 0.5 from the ring and strip envelopes and the
tick count.

The nonce predictor runs this model on the exact Fiat–Shamir shots of a given op stream. For this build's ops it
found 66 failing divisions over 32 nonces (2.1 per corpus), with nonces 0, 16 and 26 clean. This build uses
`NONCE = 0`, which passes all 9024 shots.

The modular arithmetic keeps frogtail's approximation class: 64-lane fold windows and 32-lane flag compares, each
~2^−32 per use. It uses fewer folds than frogtail.

**Limiting contract.** The PR 419 limiting contract (copied in full in §8) caps a circuit's λ at 18 on 9,024 shots
and rules out trading error directly for score. frogstrip does not build on that circuit, and its λ ≈ 2.5 is far
under the cap.

## 6. Reproduce

`cargo run --release --bin build_circuit`, or `ecdsafail run`.

The full test suite (31 tests):

    cargo test --release --bin build_circuit frogtail_batch:: -- --include-ignored --skip predict_nonce --skip mbu_bisect --skip strip_stats --skip strip_validate --skip strip_peak_sweep

It covers:
- the forward walk against the integer model on every tick;
- the regenerating walk (registers and digit word on every tick, phase 0);
- forward/inverse round trip;
- the erase for every gap 1..20;
- the four-fold shifts and both fused Horner steps, randomized against classical arithmetic;
- a 64-shot end-to-end point addition.

The nonce predictor:

    STRIP_SCAN=32 cargo test --release --bin build_circuit strip_predict_nonce -- --ignored --nocapture

All files are in `src/point_add/frogtail_batch/`:
- `frogstrip.rs`: walk, regenerating tick, unabsorb;
- `pointadd_strip.rs`: shell;
- `strip_model.rs`: integer model and schedule;
- `sched_strip.txt`, `lsc_strip.txt`: schedule and envelope tables;
- `modp_ft.rs`: table folds, fused Horner steps, four-fold shifts;
- `chunked.rs`: carry bank;
- `tests_strip.rs`: tests.
